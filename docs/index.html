the important distinction

scikit-learn is not “Python code” in one performance sense.

Its public interface is Python, but the work underneath may execute in NumPy/SciPy, BLAS/LAPACK, sklearn-owned Cython/native code, or external native libraries such as liblinear and libsvm. A useful comparison has to say which layer is being beaten.

current appraisal

The result is promising, but the interesting claim is narrower than “faster than Python.”

Flow currently wins 9 of 17 parity-eligible canonical headline comparisons. Several wins occur where sklearn already delegates substantial work to compiled code, which makes whole-estimator compilation, specialization and lower integration overhead worth investigating. But Flow loses badly on the current libsvm/liblinear-backed SVM comparisons, Lasso, KernelRidge, and several larger Digits workloads. Those are real native baselines and real optimization gaps.

What the evidence supports today: flow-scikit has competitive native implementations, strong small-workload results, a much smaller deployment model, and some end-to-end wins over layered sklearn execution. It does not yet establish universal superiority over scikit-learn, NumPy/SciPy, BLAS/LAPACK, liblinear or libsvm. Kernel speed, orchestration cost, allocation and boundary overhead are being separated explicitly.
